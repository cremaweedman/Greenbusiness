# 04 — ART BIBLE & ASSET SPEC — GREENBUSINESS v1.0

Status: CANONICAL FOR VISUAL PRODUCTION  
Scope: VS-01 and all future production art unless superseded by a higher-version canon document.

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the visual identity, production rules, asset taxonomy, export standards and review criteria for GreenBusiness.

The objective is not to imitate any existing game. GreenBusiness must read as an original management game with its own visual language and store-safe framing.

## 2. Visual North Star

GreenBusiness is a modern management game about building a fictional indoor botanical business.

The visual target is:

- premium but approachable;
- clean and highly readable;
- dark UI with controlled warm accents;
- stylized rather than photoreal;
- contemporary, not retro;
- optimistic, not criminal or gritty;
- designed for web/PWA first, then Android and iOS;
- readable at small mobile sizes;
- scalable to large quantities of future content.

Internal visual shorthand:

> Botanical Workshop Diorama — a stylized 2.5D botanical-business management game where the player's workshop visibly evolves piece by piece.

### 2.1 Canonical visual direction

The approved visual direction combines two principles:

**Botanical Workshop**
- warm, contemporary, crafted small-business atmosphere;
- practical modular equipment;
- stylized 2.5D illustration;
- matte materials and restrained accents;
- visible human-scale workspaces;
- premium through coherence and craft, not luxury.

**Management Diorama**
- gameplay spaces are composed from reusable visual modules;
- upgrades materially change the room;
- production slots remain independent from static backgrounds;
- progression should be visible without opening a stats screen;
- environment art supports gameplay state rather than baking it in.

The product should feel like a business the player is physically building, not a futuristic laboratory backdrop.

## 3. Non-negotiable identity rules

- characters;
- portraits;
- logos;
- UI layouts;
- buildings;
- missions;
- props;
- crops;

- protected visual expression

from Weeds, Weeds Social Club, or any other third-party property.

### 3.2 Store-facing framing

The product should visually communicate:

- business management;
- botanical production;
- upgrading;
- logistics;
- contracts;
- collection;
- social progression.

Avoid making the main visual identity about:

- illegal dealing;
- evading police;
- weapons;
- gangs;
- intoxication;
- drug-use paraphernalia;
- real-world cannabis cultivation instruction.

Starter crops and varieties should remain fictionalized.

## 4. Art style

### 4.1 Core style

Primary style:

**Stylized 2.5D premium game illustration**

Characteristics:

- simplified but believable forms;
- controlled materials;
- clear silhouettes;
- moderate detail;
- soft painterly/3D-inspired shading;
- limited texture noise;
- restrained highlights;
- no photoreal skin or environment rendering;
- no hyper-detailed realism that becomes inconsistent across assets.

### 4.2 Shape language

Environment:
- modular;
- squared or softly rounded industrial forms;
- clear functional zones;
- readable upgrades;
- large simple masses before micro-detail.

Props:
- compact;
- chunky enough for small-screen readability;
- 1–3 defining features per item;
- avoid decorative clutter.

Plants:
- recognizable at thumbnail size;
- fictional;
- distinct silhouettes;
- readable leaf architecture;
- controlled color accents.

Characters:
- stylized human proportions;
- expressive but not exaggerated;
- clean silhouettes;
- practical contemporary clothing;
- visually distinct role cues.

## 6. Lighting

Default:
- soft directional key;
- low-contrast ambient fill;
- warm practical lights;
- restrained green botanical accents.

Avoid:
- excessive bloom;
- cyberpunk neon;
- harsh magenta/cyan splits;
- deep crushed blacks that hide gameplay state;
- lens effects that reduce readability.

Assets should remain readable at 25% scale.

## 7. Materials

Preferred materials:

- matte painted metal;
- coated modular plastics;
- dark composite panels;
- glass only where functionally useful;
- restrained brass/gold accent pieces;
- warm wood only in limited cozy/social contexts;
- clean ceramic containers;
- fabric accents for character spaces.

Avoid:
- dirty industrial grime as a default;
- rust-heavy environments;
- chrome-heavy sci-fi surfaces;
- luxury marble/gold overload;
- cyberpunk hologram clutter.

## 8. Environment direction

### 8.1 Starter Room

The Starter Room is the first visual anchor of the product.

It must communicate:

1. small but functional business;
2. clear upgrade potential;
3. safe and organized workspace;
4. progression through visible modular improvements.

Core zones:

- production;
- storage;
- desk/management;
- contract/inbox;
- upgrade surfaces.

The room should initially feel modest, not empty.

### 8.2 Upgrade readability

Each major room upgrade should alter at least one of:

- silhouette;
- equipment density;
- surface quality;
- lighting;
- organization;
- visible technology level.

A user should perceive progression without opening a stats panel.

## 9. Production slot family

Production slots are a major gameplay asset family.

Required canonical states:

- EMPTY;
- PLANTED;
- GROWING;
- READY;
- LOCKED;
- BOOSTED;
- ATTENTION.

Rules:

- same camera/perspective for every state;
- same footprint;
- same frame silhouette;
- state changes occur inside or immediately around the slot;
- no state may rely on text;
- READY must be visibly obvious;
- ATTENTION must remain readable for color-blind players.

Recommended state cues:

EMPTY:
- open tray;
- low illumination.

PLANTED:
- seed markers / initial substrate state.

GROWING:
- visible foliage;
- active light;
- partial progress visual.

READY:
- full crop silhouette;
- brighter but controlled rim/accent;
- subtle reward/readiness indicator.

LOCKED:
- structural overlay / shutter / padlock icon.

BOOSTED:
- temporary secondary halo/icon treatment.

ATTENTION:
- warning badge/icon plus shape change.

## 10. Fictional crop design

All gameplay varieties must be fictionalized.

Each starter variety requires:

- unique silhouette;
- unique primary leaf architecture;
- unique secondary accent;
- distinct inventory icon;
- growth-stage set.

Do not use photoreal replicas of real controlled-substance cultivars.

Canonical starter visual archetypes:

### VS01-CROP-A — Broadleaf
- compact;
- rounded broad leaves;
- vivid clean green;
- beginner-friendly visual read.

### VS01-CROP-B — Spearleaf
- taller;
- narrow elongated leaves;
- muted mint-green;
- restrained warm bud/flower accent.

### VS01-CROP-C — Veinleaf
- bushier;
- medium oval leaves;
- dark green;
- subtle purple/magenta veins or undersides.

Final in-game names must come from canonical game data, not from art generation.

## 12. UI illustration system

UI art should support the interface rather than fight it.

Asset classes:

- item icons;
- contract icons;
- mastery badges;
- mission illustrations;
- upgrade illustrations;
- store pack art;
- contact portraits;
- room/state assets.

Rules:

- icon silhouette first;
- text is rendered by UI, not baked into art;
- no critical wording inside raster assets;
- maintain clean transparent margins;
- primary subject occupies 70–85% of icon canvas;
- decorative effects must not break clipping.

## 13. Iconography

Preferred icon style:

- rounded geometric;
- 2px-equivalent visual weight at target size;
- filled + cutout hybrid;
- minimal detail;
- consistent corner language.

Minimum functional icon set:

- currency;
- premium currency;
- inventory;
- crop;
- timer;
- contract;
- upgrade;
- mission;
- mastery;
- club;
- social;
- settings;
- notification;
- lock;
- ready;
- warning;
- boost.

SVG preferred for interface glyphs.

## 14. Typography

Typography remains UI-controlled.

Primary requirements:

- modern sans-serif;
- high x-height;
- strong small-size readability;
- multiple weights;
- good numeral legibility;
- no decorative display type for body text.

Current system-font/Inter approach is acceptable until final brand typography is licensed and approved.

Do not bake typography into reusable gameplay art unless the asset is explicitly a marketing/store creative.

## 15. Asset sizing and export

### UI icons
Master:
- 1024x1024

Runtime:
- 256x256 WebP/PNG as needed;
- SVG when vector-native.

### Production slots
Master:
- 1536x1536 minimum;
- transparent background;
- identical canvas and anchor across states.

Runtime:
- 512x512 WebP;
- 256x256 optional low-density variant.

### Crop inventory icons
Master:
- 1024x1024;
- transparent.

Runtime:
- 256x256 WebP.

### Character portraits
Master:
- 1600x2000 or larger;
- 4:5;
- controlled background.

Runtime:
- 640x800 WebP.

### Environment backgrounds
Master:
- 3840x2160;
- 16:9;
- keep UI-safe negative space.

Runtime:
- 1920x1080 WebP;
- responsive/mobile crops when required.

### Store art
Master:
- 1600x2000;
- 4:5.

Runtime:
- 640x800 WebP.

## 16. File formats

Source:
- layered source format where available;
- original prompt/spec metadata for AI-assisted art;
- source vector for SVG assets.

Runtime:
- WebP default for raster;
- PNG only when alpha fidelity requires it;
- SVG for interface glyphs and simple logos.

Do not ship raw source PSD/working files to web runtime bundles.

## 17. Naming convention

Pattern:

`gb_<domain>_<asset>_<state>_<variant>_vNN.ext`

Examples:

- `gb_slot_starter_empty_default_v01.webp`
- `gb_slot_starter_growing_default_v01.webp`
- `gb_crop_broadleaf_icon_default_v01.webp`
- `gb_character_alex-rowan_portrait_default_v01.webp`
- `gb_environment_starter-room_bg_default_v01.webp`

Names must be:

- lowercase;
- kebab-safe inside semantic segments;
- deterministic;
- versioned.

## 19. AI-assisted asset policy

AI generation may be used as part of the production pipeline, but generated output is not automatically production art.

Every generated asset must pass:

1. visual consistency review;
2. anatomy/object integrity review;
3. IP similarity review;
4. artifact cleanup;
5. exact crop/canvas normalization;
6. background/alpha cleanup;
7. compression/export pass;
8. in-game readability test.

Never rely on generated text inside images.

Prompt/source metadata should be retained for reproducibility where practical.

## 20. Acceptance criteria

An asset is production-approved only when:

- it matches this Art Bible;
- it is original;
- it matches required dimensions;
- naming is correct;
- alpha is clean if required;
- it works at runtime scale;
- no baked text is required for understanding;
- state is understandable without color alone where applicable;
- it has no obvious AI artifacts;
- it has no unintended logos, signatures or watermarks;
- it is referenced in the VS-01 manifest;
- it has an explicit review state of APPROVED.

## 21. VS-01 objective

VS-01 is the first coherent production-ready visual set.

It is not a full game art pack.

Its job is to prove:

- visual identity;
- gameplay readability;
- production-slot state system;
- starter crop differentiation;
- contact presentation;
- economy/mission icon direction;
- room/environment integration;
- asset pipeline repeatability.

## 22. Definition of Done for VS-01

VS-01 is complete when:

- all P0 manifest assets are APPROVED;
- no placeholder art remains in the primary five-minute loop;
- all production-slot states used by the alpha are visually supported;
- starter crops are distinguishable at inventory size;
- Alex Rowan and Mira Vale have approved portrait assets;
- the starter room has an approved production background;
- required UI/economy icons exist;
- all assets are integrated into the web client;
- frontend smoke tests still pass;
- no art asset breaks accessibility or responsive layout;
- final runtime bundle impact is measured.



## 23. Starter Room modular diorama rule

The Starter Room must be built as a layered visual system.

### Static layer
May contain:
- room shell;
- wall/floor surfaces;
- permanent architecture;
- non-interactive ambient fixtures.

### Dynamic gameplay layers
Must remain separate assets:
- production slots;
- racks;
- upgrade equipment;
- contract/inbox props if their state changes;
- storage modules if upgraded;
- temporary state indicators;
- characters or contact overlays.

### Canonical starter layout

The first room should visually support:

- Storage zone — upper/left side;
- Contract / inbox anchor — upper/right side;
- three production-slot positions — central dominant band;
- Workbench — lower/left;
- Small management desk — lower/right;
- visible unused/locked expansion potential.

The room should initially communicate:
- one functioning small business;
- low-to-mid budget equipment;
- organization without polish;
- clear physical room to improve.

### Progression principle

Major progression should alter the visible workshop through:
- more capable equipment;
- cleaner organization;
- stronger lighting hierarchy;
- denser but controlled production;
- improved surfaces;
- additional modular zones.

The visual progression arc is:

Starter workshop
→ organized workshop
→ professional botanical studio
→ mature botanical business.

Do not jump directly from starter state to futuristic lab aesthetics.
